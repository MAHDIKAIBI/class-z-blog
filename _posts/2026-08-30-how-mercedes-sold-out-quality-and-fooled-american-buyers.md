For decades, Mercedes-Benz established an unmatched global reputation for manufacturing indestructible luxury sedans engineered to outlast their owners. However, a dramatic shift during the late 1990s traded uncompromising durability for aggressive mass-market expansion, fundamentally altering the brand's legendary quality.

This analytical overview explores how intense pressure from Japanese competitors forced Mercedes-Benz to pivot away from low-volume, overbuilt engineering toward rapid volume growth. Viewers will learn how complex new electronic architecture, expanding product lines like the Alabama-built M-Class, and organizational shifts led to widespread mechanical glitches, declining quality rankings, and multi-million dollar regulatory settlements during the early 2000s.
