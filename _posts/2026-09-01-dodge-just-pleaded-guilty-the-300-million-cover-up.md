They created a three point zero liter turbocharged V six engine. It was badged as the EcoDiesel powertrain.</p><p>FCA US installed this engine inside two flagship vehicle lines. Those lines were the <span data-semantic-id="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_pickup">Ram fifteen hundred</span> pickup and the <span data-semantic-id="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep_Grand_Cherokee">Jeep Grand Cherokee</span>.</p><p>Marketing campaigns promised drivers an impossible combination of features. Advertisements claimed the engine delivered massive towing torque, best in class fuel efficiency, and clean emissions.</p><p>For years, drivers viewed diesel trucks as noisy and soot spewing work vehicles. FCA US promised that the EcoDiesel would change that perception forever. Television ads claimed the vehicles were environmentally friendly and fully compliant with federal clean air standards.</p><p>Demand surged in showrooms nationwide. Between model years twenty fourteen and twenty sixteen, the company sold over one hundred thousand EcoDiesel vehicles across North America. <a href="[YOUTUBE_VIDEO_LINK]">Check out the full documentary video to see how marketing tricked buyers.</a></p><h2>The Internal Clash with Federal Regulations</h2><p>Behind the scenes, meeting those aggressive sales claims posed an extreme engineering challenge. Federal regulations strictly limited nitrogen oxides. Nitrogen oxides are toxic gases linked directly to smog, severe asthma, and serious respiratory illnesses.</p><p>Reducing nitrogen oxides usually degrades fuel economy while simultaneously harming engine performance. Corporate executives still demanded best in class efficiency and long service intervals without sacrificing towing power.</p><p>These aggressive goals directly collided with federal environmental laws. To sell the trucks legally, the company needed certificates of conformity. The <span data-semantic-id="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Environmental_Protection_Agency">Environmental Protection Agency</span> issued these mandatory certificates.</p><p>That approval process required disclosing every single piece of software that affected emissions controls during real world driving conditions. Instead of redesigning engine hardware, management created intense internal pressure to bridge the gap through code.</p><h2>Cycle Beating by Design</h2><p>Engineers turned to software code to pass environmental certification while maintaining top performance. Deep within the engine control module, developers embedded at least eight undisclosed software functions.</p><p>Company insiders used explicit internal terms for this software strategy. They referred directly to cycle detection and cycle beating in internal communications.</p><p>Federal emissions tests rely on predictable driving patterns called cycles. These testing cycles are performed on laboratory test stands. The hidden software was calibrated to recognize the exact steering angle, vehicle speed, and ambient temperature of those government tests.</p><p>When the system recognized a test cycle, it forced emissions equipment to operate at maximum effectiveness. Nitrogen oxide levels plummeted, allowing the vehicle to easily meet clean air standards.</p><p>As soon as the truck entered ordinary road driving, the software altered its routine. Off cycle, the system dialed back pollution controls to protect fuel economy, torque, and service intervals. On the open road, toxic emissions surged far beyond legal limits.</p><p>Under the <span data-semantic-id="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Air_Act_(United_States)">Clean Air Act</span>, federal law required automakers to fully disclose all auxiliary emission control devices to regulators. Automakers understood every turn, speed, and timing parameter of those standardized testing cycles. As long as a car passed on the treadmill, regulators routinely issued a certificate of conformity.</p><p>That entire system unraveled in September twenty fifteen when the Volkswagen scandal exposed a massive blind spot in government oversight. German engineers had used secret software to cheat federal emissions tests for years.</p><p>Concerned that other automakers were doing the exact same thing, regulators changed oversight rules almost immediately. On September twenty fifth, twenty fifteen, the agency announced expanded testing across all diesel vehicles on public roads.</p><p>Inspectors strapped portable measuring equipment onto vehicles and drove them on real roads. Under normal driving conditions, actual emission numbers were quickly exposed. Both the Ram fifteen hundred and Jeep Grand Cherokee EcoDiesels produced massive spikes of nitrogen oxide emissions.</p><p>Agency engineers immediately began probing the software code hidden inside the engine control module. Regulators discovered the eight secret functions. On January twelfth, twenty seventeen, regulators officially issued a notice of violation to FCA US. The agreement also required software repairs on affected EcoDiesel vehicles.</p><p>However, those civil payments did not shield the corporate operating entity from criminal prosecution. On June third, twenty twenty two, FCA US entered a guilty plea to one felony conspiracy count. The company admitted to conspiring to defraud the United States, commit wire fraud, and violate clean air rules.</p><p>On August first, twenty twenty two, a federal judge delivered the formal sentence. The court ordered a criminal fine of ninety six million one hundred forty five thousand seven hundred eighty four dollars. The judge also ordered a criminal forfeiture of two hundred three million five hundred seventy two thousand eight hundred ninety two dollars. Combined, these criminal financial penalties totaled nearly three hundred million dollars.</p><p>Alongside the financial sentence, the court placed FCA US under three years of organizational probation. A federal grand jury indicted three key diesel managers for conspiracy and clean air violations. Those managers were Emanuele Palma, Sergio Pasini, and Gianluca Sabbioni.</p><p>Senior manager Emanuele Palma pleaded guilty in July twenty twenty three to clean air violations. Following his plea, a judge sentenced him to one day of time served after he agreed to self remove to Italy. Instead, resolution for over one hundred thousand owners meant mandatory software updates. Technicians reflashed engine control modules to remove defeat devices.</p><p>Unfortunately, these software updates created severe acceleration lag during cold starts.
